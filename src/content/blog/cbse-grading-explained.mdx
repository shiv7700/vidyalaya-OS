Every exam season, the same questions reach the school office. Is 90.5% an A1 or an A2? Why did a child with 72% overall "fail"? Why do two students both show rank 3, and nobody is rank 4? Most of the confusion comes from mixing up three different things: marks, percentages and grades. This guide explains the CBSE grading system in plain words and shows how to turn marks into a report card without mistakes.

## Marks, percentages and grades are not the same

- **Marks** are what a student scored in one paper: 42 out of 50.
- **Percentage** puts marks from papers of different sizes on the same scale: 42 out of 50 is 84%.
- **Grade** is a band that a percentage falls into: 84% is A2.

A report card usually shows all three. Trouble starts when one of them is calculated from the wrong starting point — for example, when a grade is read off raw marks from a 20-mark unit test as if it were out of 100.

## The CBSE grading scale

CBSE schools use an eight-point scale for school exams. Each grade covers a band of percentages:

This scale is for a school's own exams — unit tests, half-yearly and annual exams. The grades CBSE prints on Class 10 and Class 12 board results are worked out by the board itself, so always follow the latest CBSE circular for board classes.

## What "pass" means: 33% in each subject

The pass mark is **33% in every subject**, not 33% overall. This is the most common source of surprise for parents.

The overall percentage is a comfortable 72%, a B1. But the student scored below 33% in Mathematics, so the result is **not a pass**. The report card should make this clear, and the class teacher's remark is a good place to say what support the child needs.

In CBSE board exams, where a subject has both theory and practical parts, the board looks at each part separately. For school exams, decide your own rule at the start of the year and write it down, so every class teacher applies the same one.

## Five mistakes that creep into report cards

### 1. Grading raw marks instead of percentages

A student who scores 18 in a 20-mark test has 90%, not 18%. Always convert to a percentage first, then look up the grade. When subjects carry different maximum marks, add up the marks and the maximum marks separately, then divide — do not average the subject percentages.

### 2. Rounding without a rule

The scale uses whole numbers, but percentages often have decimals. Is 90.5% an A1 or an A2? Neither answer is wrong, but the school must pick one rule and use it everywhere. The simplest rule is not to round up: a grade starts exactly at its lower limit, so 90.5% is A2 because A1 starts at 91%.

### 3. Treating "absent" as a blank

If a student missed a paper, the record should say **AB (absent)**, not zero and not blank. A blank is easily mistaken for "marks not entered yet". An absent paper still counts as zero for that subject's pass mark unless the school holds a re-test, and the report card should show why.

### 4. Getting ranks wrong when there is a tie

If two students both have the top percentage in the section, they are both rank 1, and the next student is rank 3, not rank 2. This is called standard competition ranking (90, 80, 80, 70 gives ranks 1, 2, 2, 4). It is the fairest way to show a tie, and it is what most parents expect.

### 5. Copying totals by hand

Most errors happen when marks move from answer sheets to a register, from the register to a spreadsheet, and from the spreadsheet to a printed report card. Each copy is a chance for a typo. Enter each mark once, as close to the teacher as possible, and let everything else be calculated from it.

## A quick checklist before report cards go home

1. Every subject of the class appears on the report card, even if its marks were not entered — show "Not entered" rather than dropping the subject.
2. Maximum marks are correct for each paper.
3. Absentees are marked AB, not 0 or blank.
4. Every student below 33% in any subject is flagged, whatever their overall percentage.
5. The rounding rule for grades is the same in every section.
6. Ranks handle ties correctly.
7. Attendance for the term and the class teacher's remark are filled in.
8. The report card is signed by the class teacher and the principal.

## Keep it simple and consistent
