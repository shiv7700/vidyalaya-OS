Schools hold a lot of personal data — dates of birth, parents' phone numbers, attendance, marks, fees, staff salaries — and most of it is about children. India's **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** (the DPDP Act) sets out how organisations must handle such data, and it has specific rules for children. This article explains, in plain words, what the DPDP Act means for schools and what to ask your software vendor.

This is a general explanation, not legal advice. The Act is supported by rules made by the government, and these rules and their timelines can change. Check the latest DPDP Rules and speak to a lawyer about your school's situation.

## The key terms

- **Personal data** — any data about a person who can be identified from it: a name, a mobile number, a roll number with a class, marks.
- **Data Principal** — the person the data is about. For a child, the parent or lawful guardian acts on the child's behalf.
- **Data Fiduciary** — the organisation that decides why and how the data is used. For student, parent and staff records, **that is the school**.
- **Data Processor** — an organisation that handles data on the fiduciary's behalf, such as your school management software provider.

The Act covers personal data in digital form, and data collected on paper that is later digitised. If your admission forms are typed into a computer, they are covered.

## Children's data: the most important part for schools

Under the DPDP Act, a **child is anyone under 18**. That means almost every student in a school, including Class 11 and 12 students. Section 9 of the Act says a Data Fiduciary:

- must obtain **verifiable consent from a parent or lawful guardian** before processing a child's personal data;
- must not process a child's data in a way likely to harm the child's well-being;
- must not carry out **tracking or behavioural monitoring of children**, or **targeted advertising directed at children**.

The Act allows the government to exempt certain organisations or purposes from some of these conditions, and the DPDP Rules include limited exemptions that can apply to educational institutions — for example, for monitoring connected to educational activities or to children's safety. These exemptions are narrow and depend on their exact wording, so check with your lawyer which of your activities they cover.

In practice, most schools will need to:

1. Update the admission form to explain clearly what data the school collects, why, and who it is shared with.
2. Get the parent's consent in a way that can be proved later, with a record of who gave it and when.
3. Make sure no one uses students' data for advertising — including through apps, websites or vendors the school works with.

The school stays responsible even when a vendor processes the data, and should engage processors only under a valid contract.

## Rights of parents, students and staff

Data Principals — and parents on behalf of children — have the right to:

- **access** a summary of their data and how it is processed;
- have it **corrected, completed or updated**, and **erased** when it is no longer needed;
- have their **grievances** addressed by the school;
- **nominate** someone to exercise these rights if they die or become unable to.

If a person is not satisfied with how the school handled their grievance, they can go to the **Data Protection Board of India**.

## Penalties

The Act sets high maximum penalties, decided by the Data Protection Board:

- up to **₹250 crore** for failing to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach;
- up to **₹200 crore** for failing to meet the additional obligations for children's data;
- up to **₹200 crore** for failing to report a breach to the Board and affected people.

These are maximums: the Board weighs how serious the breach was and what the organisation did to limit harm.

## A starting checklist for schools

1. **Map your data.** List what you collect, where it is kept (registers, spreadsheets, software, WhatsApp groups), and who can see it.
2. **Collect less.** If a field on the admission form is never used, remove it.
3. **Update notices and consent** on admission forms, and keep proof of consent.
4. **Limit access.** Teachers should see their own classes, parents only their own children, and staff who leave should lose access the same day.
5. **Choose a grievance contact** and publish their details for parents and staff.
6. **Set retention rules** — how long you keep records of students who have left, enquiries that did not join, and so on.
7. **Review your vendors** and sign proper data processing terms with each.

## What to ask your school software vendor

- Where is our data stored, and who hosts it?
- How is our school's data kept separate from other schools on the same system?
- Do you use tracking, analytics or advertising on pages our students and parents use?
- Will you tell us promptly if there is a breach?
- Can we export all our data, and what happens to it if we stop using your service?
- Do you act only on our instructions as a Data Processor, and is that in the contract?
